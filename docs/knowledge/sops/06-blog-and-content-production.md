# Blog and Content Production

## Purpose

This SOP defines how Max plans, drafts, reviews, stores, and publishes content for a client.

Content should support a real customer need, search intent, local relevance, or approved business goal. Max must not create content only to increase volume.

## Content types

This SOP applies to:

- Blogs
- Service pages
- Location pages
- Website pages
- Google Business Profile posts
- Social posts
- Email drafts

Each content record must identify its content type and intended publication destination.

## Topic selection

Max may propose topics. The agency owner may also provide a topic directly.

Topics should be based on the applicable combination of:

- Search intent
- Local relevance
- Approved client services
- Real customer questions
- Seasonal demand
- Business goals
- Existing content gaps
- Available evidence

Max should avoid topics that duplicate existing client content or target services the client does not provide.

Max may reuse a general topic for another client only when the services, facts, audience, location, and final content are meaningfully different. It must not copy one client's content or facts into another client's record.

## Content brief

Before drafting substantial content, Max creates a brief containing:

- Client
- Content type
- Proposed title
- Primary keyword
- Secondary keywords
- Search intent
- Target location
- Reader
- Business goal
- H1
- H2 outline
- Internal-link targets
- Call to action
- Source facts
- Image recommendation
- Acceptance criteria

Routine short content may use a compact brief, but the source facts and requested outcome must still be clear.

## Keywords and SEO

Max must preserve client-approved keywords when they are relevant and natural.

Max may suggest new keywords, but new strategic keywords require approval before being treated as part of the client's approved SEO profile.

If a keyword conflicts with the client's actual services, locations, or approved facts, Max must not use it as a factual claim. It should flag the conflict and propose a safer alternative.

Content should follow the applicable Local SEO skills and SOPs, including:

- Search intent
- Local service relevance
- Service-area accuracy
- H1 and H2 structure
- Internal linking
- Metadata
- Local business information
- Structured data when applicable
- Accessibility

## Client facts and claims

Every factual claim must be supported by the official client profile, approved source material, or clearly labeled live or imported evidence.

Max must not invent:

- Services
- Locations
- Prices
- Reviews
- Testimonials
- Certifications
- Awards
- Rankings
- Results
- Guarantees
- Customer counts
- Years in business
- Business history

Phone numbers, service areas, and business details may be included when they are verified and useful for the content.

## Voice and writing

Content uses the approved client brand voice when one exists.

When no client voice exists, use the default voice from the Universal Human Writing SOP:

- Direct
- Warm without exaggeration
- Clear
- Practical
- Conversational without forced slang
- Appropriate for the reader and channel

The Universal Human Writing SOP runs before content is submitted for approval or publication.

## Content length and structure

Content length depends on the content type and reader need:

- Blogs: detailed enough to answer the search intent without filler.
- Service pages: focused on the service and conversion path.
- Location pages: genuinely localized, not copied pages with only a city name changed.
- Google posts: concise and action-oriented.
- Social posts: short and channel-appropriate.
- Emails: direct and focused on one action.

Use one H1 per webpage. Use H2 headings for major sections. Keep paragraphs readable on mobile devices.

## Calls to action and linking

Blogs, service pages, location pages, and Google Business Profile posts should include a relevant call to action when one is supported by the client facts.

Max should link content to relevant approved service pages or contact paths when those destinations exist.

Do not add links to pages that do not exist or are not approved.

## Images and assets

Max may recommend images, photo subjects, alt text, or asset placement.

Max should not invent that a client owns an image or has a particular asset. Generated images require a separate approved image workflow.

Use client-provided or approved assets when available.

## Missing information

Max chooses based on risk:

- Major claim, service, location, offer, or external publication: ask or omit the claim.
- Low-risk structure or formatting: continue without the missing detail and record what is unknown.
- Uncertain client-specific fact: never fill it with a generic claim that implies it is true.

## Drafting and approval

Scheduled content may publish automatically when the client has enabled the approved blog-publishing workflow and all factual, SEO, and publication checks pass.

Blogs, service pages, and location pages require approval before external publication unless the client has enabled a pre-approved scheduled blog workflow.

Google Business Profile posts require approval unless the client has enabled the pre-approved scheduled-post workflow defined in the Google Business Profile SOP.

Codex may create or update draft content files in the client's GitHub project when the task packet permits it. The packet must include the content brief, client facts, SEO requirements, and required checks.

Approved content may be published through the authorized workflow. Publication must record the destination, version, time, and result.

Google Business Profile posts always require explicit approval before publishing.

## Rejections and revisions

When content is rejected or changes are requested:

1. Preserve the rejected version.
2. Record the requested changes and reason when available.
3. Create a new content version.
4. Re-run the relevant checks.
5. Request approval for the new version.

Do not silently replace a rejected or published artifact.

## Duplicate prevention

Max should prevent duplicate topics and substantially duplicate content for the same client.

Before drafting, check the client's existing content history.

Similar topics may be used when the search intent, audience, service, location, or requested outcome is materially different.

## Model and cost routing

Use the efficient model for:

- Routine blog drafts
- Formatting
- Metadata
- Short Google posts
- Summaries
- Content adaptation using an approved brief

Use the balanced or strongest model for:

- Topic strategy
- Difficult search intent
- Conflicting facts
- High-value landing pages
- Complex local SEO planning
- Content that could create significant client risk

Every substantial content task should include an estimated cost. Use code for validation, duplicate checks, word counts, links, and required-field checks instead of using AI.

## Recordkeeping

For every substantial content artifact, record:

- Client
- Content type
- Title
- Target keyword
- Search intent
- Source facts
- SOP and skill versions
- Prompt or work-packet version
- Model role
- Draft
- Humanized version
- Human edits
- Approval or rejection
- Rejection reason when provided
- Final location
- Publication result
- Verification result

## Final checklist

- Is the content for the correct client?
- Is the content type identified?
- Is the topic useful and non-duplicative?
- Is search intent clear?
- Are keywords relevant and approved?
- Are claims supported by facts?
- Are service areas accurate?
- Is the brand voice appropriate?
- Did the Universal Human Writing SOP run?
- Are headings and internal links correct?
- Is the call to action supported?
- Are images and assets approved or clearly labeled as recommendations?
- Was the correct model role used?
- Is approval required and recorded?
- Is the final version preserved?
- Is publication verified?
