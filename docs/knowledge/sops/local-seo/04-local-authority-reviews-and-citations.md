# Local authority, reviews, and citations SOP

## Purpose

This SOP covers NAP consistency, citation audits, citation correction, authentic review generation, review replies, review monitoring, local relationships, and relevant links.

## Canonical identity record

Before citation or review work, confirm:

- approved business name
- public address or hidden-address status
- primary phone
- approved website
- primary category
- approved hours
- location identifier
- prior names
- prior addresses
- prior phone numbers
- tracking numbers
- owner of each listing

Do not start bulk submissions without an approved identity record.

## NAP consistency

NAP means name, address, and phone.

The goal is a clear, accurate business identity across important sources.

Prioritize:

1. wrong business
2. wrong or old address
3. wrong phone
4. wrong website
5. duplicate listing
6. incorrect location status
7. private address exposed
8. inconsistent public name that may confuse customers
9. minor formatting differences

Do not report an accuracy percentage unless the tested listings and scoring method are documented.

## Citation classes

### Core platforms

Examples include:

- Google Business Profile
- Apple Business Connect
- Bing Places
- major social or map platforms used by the client

### Industry sources

Use legitimate sources that customers, regulators, associations, or professionals in the industry use.

### Local sources

Examples include:

- chamber of commerce
- city or regional business organizations
- local media
- local associations
- local sponsorship pages
- verified partner pages

### General directories

Use only credible directories with a real customer or entity purpose.

Do not chase citation count through low-quality sites.

## Citation audit process

For each listing, record:

- platform
- listing URL
- location
- claimed status
- current name
- current address
- current phone
- current website
- current category
- duplicate status
- accuracy result
- source date
- correction method
- login owner
- risk
- priority
- evidence

Status values:

- correct
- materially incorrect
- minor difference
- duplicate
- old location
- missing
- inaccessible
- pending
- corrected
- verified

## Citation correction

Priority:

1. core platform errors
2. duplicate or old-location records
3. wrong phone or website
4. major industry sources
5. major local sources
6. high-visibility general directories
7. lower-priority sources

Before submission:

- capture the current listing
- confirm approved replacement values
- obtain approval
- use the platform's correction path
- store confirmation
- verify the live result later

Do not create a new listing when a correct listing can be claimed or fixed.

Do not publish a fake address to make a citation appear local.

Do not use one location's NAP for another location.

## Tracking numbers

A tracking number may be used when:

- the client owns or controls it
- routing is tested
- the canonical number is preserved in approved records
- the implementation does not misrepresent the location
- reporting rules are documented
- emergency and after-hours behavior is known

Do not replace the only known canonical number across the web without a migration plan.

## Review strategy

The goal is a steady process for requesting honest feedback from real customers.

### Allowed

- ask a real customer for an honest review
- ask after a real service interaction
- provide a direct review link
- remind a customer without pressure
- ask all eligible customers under a consistent rule
- monitor review platforms
- respond professionally
- report content that appears to violate platform policy

### Prohibited

- fake reviews
- purchased reviews
- employee, contractor, friend, or family reviews presented as independent customer experiences
- reviews written by the business for the customer
- review gating
- asking only customers expected to leave positive reviews
- discounts, gifts, contests, or other prohibited incentives tied to reviews
- instructing reviewers to include keywords, cities, services, or a star rating
- review kiosks or tactics intended to manipulate identity signals
- threats or pressure
- retaliation
- fabricated review screenshots
- moving reviews between locations without clear attribution

## Review request workflow

1. Define an eligible customer event.
2. Confirm the customer actually received the service.
3. Confirm the platform allows solicitation.
4. Send one plain request for honest feedback.
5. Provide the correct location-specific link.
6. Do not suggest wording or rating.
7. Record request time and channel.
8. Apply a reasonable reminder rule.
9. provide an opt-out.
10. Monitor for delivery errors.

Example request goal:

```text
Ask the customer to share an honest review of their experience. Do not ask for five stars and do not suggest service or location phrases.
```

Run the Human Writing SOP on the final request.

## Review responses

### General rules

- respond as the correct business and location
- thank the reviewer without scripted enthusiasm
- reference details only when safe and supplied
- do not add keywords for ranking
- do not argue
- do not disclose private information
- do not promise a remedy that has not been approved
- move account-specific resolution offline
- vary wording
- record the response and approval

### Positive reviews

A useful response may:

- thank the reviewer
- acknowledge the service mentioned
- recognize a team member when approved
- invite the customer back when appropriate

### Negative reviews

A useful response should:

- acknowledge the feedback
- avoid confirming disputed facts publicly
- avoid admissions that require legal review
- explain the next safe contact step
- remain calm
- escalate serious claims

### Regulated or sensitive situations

Escalate responses involving:

- health information
- legal representation
- financial information
- minors
- discrimination
- safety incidents
- fraud
- threats
- personal data
- active litigation
- criminal allegations

Do not confirm a protected relationship or disclose service records.

## Fake or policy-violating review handling

1. Save evidence.
2. Check the platform's current policy.
3. Flag through the approved process.
4. Record the reason.
5. Respond only when a public reply is useful and approved.
6. Do not accuse a named competitor without proof.
7. Monitor the decision.
8. Escalate legal issues.

Do not claim that a review is fake merely because it is negative.

## Review reporting

Track:

- total review count by platform
- average rating
- new reviews by period
- review request count
- review conversion rate when measurable
- response rate
- median response time
- recurring positive themes
- recurring complaint themes
- unresolved serious issues
- platform restrictions or removed reviews

Do not claim that a review caused a ranking change.

## Local relationships and links

Legitimate opportunities may include:

- chambers
- trade associations
- local organizations
- suppliers
- verified partners
- community projects
- scholarships with a real purpose
- sponsorships with real participation
- local media coverage
- professional directories
- manufacturer provider directories

Rules:

- the relationship must be real
- the page must be relevant
- payment must be disclosed in internal records
- do not buy links solely to manipulate rankings
- do not exchange links at scale
- do not use exact-match anchor requirements
- do not guarantee a link or ranking result
- require approval before outreach or payment
- verify the live link and destination

## Completion standard

This SOP is complete when:

- the canonical identity record is approved
- priority citations were audited
- corrections have approval
- review requests are policy-safe
- no review gating or incentives exist
- review replies protect privacy
- local relationships are legitimate
- evidence and verification are recorded
- no ranking or customer result was invented
