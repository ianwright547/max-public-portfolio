# Google Business Profile SOP

## Purpose

This SOP covers profile eligibility, business information, categories, services, service areas, NAP alignment, hours, descriptions, photos, posts, questions, change control, and verification.

When producing the client-facing audit, also use the GBP and Landing Page Recommendation template. Include the GBP URL, landing page URL, location, recommended landing page, recommended H1, and recommended title tag before listing the landing-page and profile recommendations.

Follow current Google policy before changing a live profile.

## Risk rule

A Google Business Profile is a public business record. Incorrect changes can confuse customers, reduce visibility, or cause restrictions.

All live changes require recorded approval.

Take a before snapshot before changing:

- business name
- primary category
- additional categories
- address visibility
- service areas
- phone
- website
- hours
- description
- services
- attributes
- profile status
- recent edits
- linked accounts

## Step 1: Confirm eligibility and business type

Classify the business:

- `storefront`: customers are served at a real, staffed location
- `service_area`: the business travels to customers and does not serve them at its address
- `hybrid`: customers are served at the address and the business also travels to customers
- `ineligible_or_unclear`: facts do not support a profile or more evidence is required

For a service-area business:

- use the real business base for verification
- hide the address when customers are not served there
- do not use a mailbox, unstaffed virtual office, or borrowed address
- keep one profile for the central operation unless separate eligible locations have separate staff and operations
- use accurate service areas

A website location page does not make an address eligible.

## Step 2: Verify the business name

The profile name must match the real-world name used on approved business materials and customer-facing records.

Do not add:

- services
- cities
- counties
- hours
- slogans
- phone numbers
- legal phrases not used publicly
- unnecessary punctuation
- promotional claims

Record the legal name, public name, and approved profile name separately.

Do not change the name to gain keywords.

## Step 3: Verify address and service areas

For storefront and hybrid businesses:

- confirm the location is real
- confirm it is staffed during stated customer-facing hours
- confirm permanent business identification exists when required
- confirm customers can be received there
- confirm suite and access details
- confirm the map pin

For service-area businesses:

- keep the private address out of public copy and public structured data unless the owner explicitly approves publication and policy allows it
- list only places the business actually serves
- remove places the business no longer serves
- do not select a whole state when the operation covers a smaller region
- do not treat service areas as ranking radius controls

Store evidence for each eligible physical location.

## Step 4: Verify phone and website

Phone:

- must reach the correct business or location
- should use an approved local or business-controlled number
- may use tracking when ownership, routing, and canonical NAP rules are documented
- must not route to an unrelated lead seller
- must be tested

Website:

- must represent the same business and location
- should point to the most relevant approved page
- must not redirect to an unrelated entity
- should use approved tracking parameters when reporting requires them
- must be tested on mobile and desktop
- should use HTTPS

## Step 5: Select categories

Google advises businesses to use the fewest categories needed to describe the core business and to choose specific categories that complete the statement, "This business is a."

Process:

1. List valid current categories.
2. Match each category to an actual operating service.
3. Compare current profile categories with approved services.
4. Review relevant competitors as evidence, not as authority.
5. Choose one primary category that best represents the main business.
6. Add only secondary categories that are accurate and meaningful.
7. Document why each category applies.
8. Record before and after values.
9. Obtain approval.
10. Verify the profile after the change.

Do not add a category because a competitor uses it.

Do not add categories for future or occasional services that the business does not currently offer.

Do not promise a ranking increase from a category change.

## Step 6: Manage services

Build services from approved operations.

For each service, store:

- service name
- parent category
- approved description
- price or price range, when approved
- availability
- service area
- linked website page
- status

Service rules:

- use clear customer language
- remove exact and near duplicates
- do not stack city names into service names
- do not repeat synonyms only for keywords
- do not add phone numbers or unsupported promotional text
- do not add services the business cannot fulfill
- map important services to useful website pages
- verify current Google restrictions before publishing custom services

A service entry is not a substitute for a complete service page when the intent needs more information.

## Step 7: Maintain canonical NAP

Maintain one approved identity record:

- business name
- public address or hidden-address status
- phone
- website
- hours
- primary category

Prioritize material mismatches:

- wrong business name
- old address
- wrong phone
- wrong website
- duplicate profile
- wrong location
- private address exposed
- different business attached to the record

Minor formatting differences such as "Street" and "St" are lower priority when the identity remains clear. Do not create unnecessary work solely to make punctuation identical.

## Step 8: Write the business description

Use only approved facts.

A useful description may include:

- what the business is
- main services
- actual service area
- verified differentiators
- relevant business history
- a plain next step

Do not include:

- misleading claims
- unsupported "best" or "number one" language
- promotions or sales-focused pricing
- links
- keyword lists
- fake history
- fake locations
- ranking claims
- unapproved guarantees
- excessive place names
- all caps or gimmicky characters

Run the Universal Human Writing SOP before approval.

## Step 9: Set accurate hours and attributes

Hours must reflect real customer access or real staffed availability.

Do not extend hours only because open businesses may appear differently in results.

Maintain:

- regular hours
- holiday hours
- seasonal closures
- appointment-only rules
- service-specific hours, when supported
- emergency availability, when true

Complete only applicable attributes. Marking an attribute incorrectly is a misleading business statement.

## Step 10: Recommend photos and other assets

Recommend real, current, rights-cleared assets such as:

- exterior identification for eligible storefronts
- entrance and parking information
- team members
- branded vehicles
- equipment
- work in progress
- completed work
- products
- service process
- owner or manager
- location-specific assets
- before-and-after sets when accurate

Rules:

- do not use stock photos as proof of completed work
- do not fabricate customers, locations, equipment, or results
- obtain permission for identifiable people and private property
- remove sensitive customer information
- preserve accurate before-and-after conditions
- do not add false location metadata
- do not promise ranking improvement from metadata
- use accurate captions and filenames
- store ownership and permission status

## Step 11: Posts and questions

Profile posts:

- use approved offers, services, updates, or educational topics
- keep claims accurate
- use the correct destination
- record publication date and URL
- review platform policy
- run the Human Writing SOP
- require approval before publishing

Questions and answers:

- answer real questions accurately
- do not pose as a customer
- do not publish private information
- do not invent policies or availability
- move detailed educational material to the website when appropriate
- monitor for spam or misinformation

## Step 12: Profile change control

Every proposed change must include:

- field
- current value
- proposed value
- reason
- source
- expected customer benefit
- ranking claim class
- policy risk
- approver
- rollback value

Avoid changing several high-impact fields at once unless needed to correct false information. Separate changes when measurement matters.

## Step 13: Verify

After execution:

- confirm the public field
- confirm profile status
- check for rejected or pending edits
- test phone and website
- confirm private address handling
- capture evidence
- record verification time
- schedule a later check when propagation is delayed

Do not mark the change verified from an API success response alone.

## Suspension and restriction risk

Escalate when there is:

- a fake or unclear location
- name stuffing
- an unstaffed office
- a duplicate
- a recent move
- a rebrand
- a practitioner or department conflict
- a rejected verification
- a restriction notice
- suspicious review activity
- ownership conflict

Do not make aggressive changes to "fix ranking" while eligibility is unresolved.

## Completion standard

The profile SOP is complete when:

- eligibility is confirmed
- the approved identity record exists
- categories and services are accurate
- service areas are truthful
- description and assets are approved
- live changes have approval
- before and after evidence is stored
- public results are verified
- limitations are recorded
