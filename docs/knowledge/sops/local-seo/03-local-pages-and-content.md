# Local pages and content SOP

## Purpose

This SOP supports the roadmap phases. It does not create pages by arbitrary volume. Page decisions must follow measured search intent, approved services, GBP structure, and the current rank-map phase.

This SOP covers page mapping, service pages, physical location pages, service-area pages, blog posts, titles, headings, opening copy, internal links, local proof, and content maintenance.

Blog posts are part of this system. They must serve a real reader and a defined search purpose.

## Required facts

Before writing, load:

- approved business facts
- approved services
- approved places
- prohibited claims
- brand voice
- current page inventory
- keyword and intent map
- internal link inventory
- verified proof
- required calls to action
- Universal Human Writing SOP

Do not invent local detail to make a page feel unique.

## Page roles

### Homepage or GBP landing page

Use it to establish:

- what the business is
- main service
- primary market
- actual availability
- main differentiators
- main action
- links to priority categories, services, and locations

### Category page

Use it when several services belong to one meaningful category.

### Service page

Use it for a distinct service with clear transactional or commercial intent.

### Emergency service page

Use it only when the business can fulfill the urgency described.

### Physical location page

Use it for a real, eligible customer-facing location.

It may include:

- approved NAP
- hours
- parking or access
- location team
- services available there
- real photos
- directions
- verified local reviews
- unique structured data

### Service-area page

Use it for a place the business serves without implying an office.

State service coverage honestly. Do not publish a fake address, fake team, fake local phone, or false "located in" language.

### Service and place page

Use it when a specific service and place have distinct intent and enough unique value.

### Blog post

Use it for educational, comparative, seasonal, regulatory, project-based, or decision-focused content.

### FAQ

Use it to answer real recurring questions. Do not create dozens of thin pages for individual questions when one useful page can answer them.

## Page creation decision

### Core 30 decision

Use Core 30 only when the Local SEO Roadmap supports it, such as a competitive market, high-value industry, strong competitors with comprehensive coverage, or a 10–15% top-three coverage plateau after 8–10 weeks of correct foundation work.

The Core 30 hierarchy mirrors the GBP:

```text
Homepage: primary category + city
    ↓
Category pages: secondary GBP categories
    ↓
Service pages: services under each category
```

Every page must correspond to an approved category or service and contain genuinely specific content. Stop expanding when measured topical coverage is sufficient, commonly around 30–40% top-three coverage, unless a documented business goal supports more.

### Geographic expansion decision

After topical foundation is strong and top-three coverage reaches approximately 30–40%, evaluate neighborhood, city, suburb, and service-area pages. Each page must provide unique local value and must not be a city-swapped doorway page.

Create a new page only when:

- it has a distinct purpose
- the intent is not already served well
- the business can fulfill the promise
- the content can be useful and specific
- it fits the site hierarchy
- it can receive internal links
- it can be maintained
- it does not create duplicate targeting

Otherwise:

- improve an existing page
- merge overlapping pages
- add a section
- use a blog post
- use a profile service
- take no action

## Content sequence

Use this flexible sequence when it fits the intent:

1. Identify the service and place.
2. Explain the immediate next step.
3. State useful approved differentiators.
4. Explain what is included.
5. Address important questions, risks, timing, or price factors.
6. Provide proof.
7. Link to related services or areas.
8. Give a clear call to action.

Do not force every page into the same visible structure.

## Titles

A title should:

- match the primary page purpose
- include the service and place when natural and useful
- distinguish the page from other pages
- remain readable
- avoid repeated keyword variants
- avoid unsupported claims
- fit the actual content

Do not use "Home" as the title for a main local landing page.

Do not force the brand to the front when the service and place are more useful to the searcher.

## H1

Use one clear H1 by default.

The H1 should identify the page accurately. It may include the service and place. It does not need to match the title word for word.

Avoid slogans that hide what the page is about.

## Opening paragraph

The opening should help the visitor confirm:

- what the business does
- where the service is available
- whether the page fits their need
- what to do next
- why the business may be a fit

Do not open with a long company history when the visitor needs service information.

Do not repeat the title and H1 mechanically.

## Service page requirements

Include as appropriate:

- service definition
- problems it addresses
- what is included
- who it is for
- process
- timing factors
- price factors
- exclusions
- qualifications
- warranty or guarantee terms, if approved
- service area
- proof
- FAQs
- related services
- call to action

Do not claim a response time, price, warranty, or result without an approved basis.

## Physical location page requirements

Use only for a real location.

Include as appropriate:

- exact approved NAP
- hours
- services available at the location
- location team
- real local assets
- access information
- local proof
- nearby landmarks for customer navigation
- location-specific contact path
- map
- unique structured data
- internal links to relevant services

Do not use one location's reviews or team as though they belong to another location.

## Service-area page requirements

Use truthful language such as:

- serving customers in
- mobile service available in
- appointments available across
- travel availability for

Avoid false language such as:

- our office in
- our local branch
- located in
- visit us in

unless a real eligible location exists.

Useful unique material may include:

- services actually offered there
- travel or scheduling details
- local conditions
- local regulations
- verified work examples
- approved customer feedback
- common problems in the area
- related nearby service areas

A place-name swap is not unique content.

## Blog strategy

### Approved blog purposes

- answer customer questions
- explain costs and decisions
- address local seasonal conditions
- explain local rules
- document approved projects
- compare service options
- support service and location pages
- provide a useful source for outreach
- refresh outdated information
- respond to new Search Console demand

### Blog topic test

Before approval, answer:

- Who is this for?
- What question does it answer?
- What evidence supports it?
- Which service or location does it support?
- What page should it link to?
- What action should the reader take?
- Why is a blog post better than updating an existing page?

### Cadence

A weekly cadence is allowed. Do not publish a weak article because the calendar says a post is due.

When a new article would add little value, choose one of these:

- refresh an existing article
- expand a service page
- update an FAQ
- add verified project evidence
- improve internal links
- consolidate overlapping posts

### Blog quality

Do not use arbitrary word counts as a quality rule.

A finished post should:

- answer the question
- use approved facts
- distinguish facts from opinion
- cite external claims when needed
- include local context only when real
- link to useful internal pages
- avoid filler
- include an update date when facts can change
- pass the Human Writing SOP
- contain no em dashes

## Internal linking

Build a browsable hierarchy.

Typical pattern:

```text
homepage or GBP landing page
  -> category pages
      -> service pages
          -> relevant location pages
          -> supporting blog posts
```

Internal link rules:

- link based on topic and user need
- use descriptive natural anchor text
- avoid repeating the same exact anchor everywhere
- link each important page from at least one relevant indexable page
- keep priority pages within a reasonable click path
- use breadcrumbs when appropriate
- link blog posts to the service or location they support
- link service pages to useful supporting blogs
- link location pages to services available there
- cross-link nearby areas only when useful
- do not put every link on every page
- do not create hidden links
- fix broken links
- remove links to deleted or redirected pages
- record important link changes

## Local proof

Allowed proof must be approved and traceable:

- real reviews
- real project examples
- real certifications
- real awards
- real years in business
- real team experience
- real warranties
- real response policies
- real service statistics
- real partnerships
- real local involvement

Do not create proof from competitor patterns or general industry expectations.

## Keyword use

Use the main topic in prominent places when it reads naturally:

- title
- H1
- opening copy
- relevant subheadings
- image alt text when the image supports it
- internal link text
- metadata
- structured data fields that match visible facts

Do not:

- list cities in blocks
- repeat the same service phrase unnaturally
- rotate synonyms without purpose
- hide keywords
- add unrelated services
- write for a detector
- state that a page is optimized before the checklist is complete

## Content updates and consolidation

Audit content for:

- stale facts
- overlapping intent
- duplicate pages
- weak internal links
- no impressions
- irrelevant traffic
- poor conversions
- broken links
- unsupported claims
- outdated local rules
- missing proof

A page with low traffic is not automatically useless.

Before deleting or merging, review:

- backlinks
- conversions
- assisted conversions
- Search Console queries
- seasonality
- internal links
- current rankings
- page purpose
- replacement destination

Use an approved redirect when a page has a valid replacement. Return the correct status when it does not.

## Human writing pass

Before final copy:

- load the Human Writing SOP
- confirm the speaker and reader
- preserve approved facts
- remove generic marketing language
- remove keyword repetition
- vary sentence structure naturally
- keep useful local detail
- remove false urgency
- remove unsupported superlatives
- remove chatbot commentary
- confirm no em dashes
- run the publication checklist

## Completion standard

A page or post is ready for approval when:

- its purpose and intent are documented
- every service and place is approved
- local proof is traceable
- the title and H1 are clear
- the opening supports goal completion
- internal links are mapped
- metadata is drafted
- structured data needs are noted
- unsupported claims are removed
- the Human Writing SOP passed
- the publication checklist passed
