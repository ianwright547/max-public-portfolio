# Local SEO research and strategy SOP

## Purpose

This SOP covers local keyword research, search intent, service-area targeting, location strategy, page mapping, priority scoring, and local ranking limitations.

Use the Local SEO Roadmap and Phase Gates SOP first. Measurement and the current roadmap phase determine whether the client needs foundation work, trust building, Core 30 topical coverage, or geographic expansion.

Use it before creating pages, changing a Google Business Profile, or proposing ongoing content.

## Inputs

Required when available:

- approved business services
- approved business categories
- approved service areas
- business type
- verified business location status
- revenue or priority services, when approved
- hours and response capacity
- licensing or jurisdiction limits
- existing website pages
- Google Business Profile services
- Search Console queries
- analytics and conversion data
- geogrid or local rank data
- competitor examples
- customer language from calls, forms, reviews, and intake
- seasonal constraints
- current rank-map top-three coverage
- rank-map query, grid, date, and provider settings
- current roadmap phase

Record the date and source of every dataset.

## Step 1: Confirm operational truth

Before keyword work, confirm what the business can actually fulfill.

For each service, record:

- official service name
- customer wording
- included work
- excluded work
- residential, commercial, or both
- emergency availability
- service hours
- qualifications or licensing limits
- price or quote policy, if approved
- places where the service is available
- places where it is not available
- business priority

Do not research or target a service that the business does not offer.

## Step 2: Build the seed set

Create seeds from:

- approved services
- primary and secondary categories
- products or procedures people request by name
- customer problems and symptoms
- urgent needs
- cost and estimate questions
- qualification questions
- brands or equipment the business is authorized to service
- common customer language
- local regulations or seasonal issues
- existing Search Console queries
- existing successful pages

Keep industry terms and customer terms separate when they mean different things.

## Step 3: Expand query patterns

Research these query groups where relevant:

### Core service queries

Examples:

- mobile mechanic
- drain cleaning
- interior detailing
- ceramic coating

### Explicit local queries

Examples:

- mobile mechanic Orlando
- drain cleaning in Phoenix
- ceramic coating near Dr. Phillips

### Implicit local queries

Some service queries trigger local results without a place name. Confirm the current result page before classifying them.

### Near-me queries

Treat near-me searches as location-dependent queries. Do not insert "near me" repeatedly into page copy. Strong eligibility, relevance, prominence, and actual distance matter more than repeating the phrase.

### Problem and symptom queries

Use the words customers use before they know the service name.

### Urgent queries

Examples include emergency, same-day, open now, and 24-hour. Use only when the business can honestly meet the stated availability.

### Cost and decision queries

Examples include price, cost, repair versus replacement, what is included, and how long the work takes.

### Qualification queries

Examples include licensed, insured, financing, warranty, commercial, fleet, accepts a specific plan, or works on a certain product. Every qualification must be approved.

### Informational queries

Use these for blogs, guides, FAQs, or supporting sections when they help a local customer make a decision.

### Conversational queries

Write natural questions a person may ask a search assistant. Treat measured demand as uncertain unless a reliable tool provides it.

## Step 4: Collect evidence

Possible sources:

- Search Console
- Google Ads Keyword Planner
- approved keyword tools
- live result pages
- autocomplete
- People Also Ask
- competitor pages
- Google Business Profile categories and services
- customer call notes
- form submissions
- support questions
- reviews
- industry forums

Tool volume and difficulty values are estimates. Store the tool, country, place setting, date, and match type.

Do not invent search volume, cost-per-click, difficulty, traffic, or conversion rate.

## Step 5: Classify search intent

Assign one primary intent and optional secondary intent.

| Intent | Meaning | Common output |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | Immediate help is needed | emergency service page, accurate GBP hours |
| Transactional | Ready to contact, book, buy, or request a quote | service or location page |
| Commercial investigation | Comparing providers, methods, prices, or proof | service page, comparison page, case study, blog |
| Informational | Learning or troubleshooting | blog, guide, FAQ |
| Navigational | Looking for a known business or page | homepage, contact page, GBP |
| Local discovery | Looking for a provider nearby | GBP and main local landing page |
| Qualification | Checking eligibility, financing, licensing, or compatibility | service page or qualification section |

Do not choose a page type from volume alone.

## Step 6: Cluster by meaning and result overlap

Group queries when they share:

- the same customer need
- the same likely next action
- similar result pages
- the same service
- the same page purpose

Do not create separate pages for small wording changes.

Split a cluster when:

- the service is operationally different
- the search intent is different
- the business serves a different customer type
- the expected answer is materially different
- the current result pages show separate page types
- a distinct place has enough unique value for its own page

## Step 7: Map each cluster to the right asset

Possible assignments:

- homepage or GBP landing page
- category page
- service page
- emergency service page
- physical location page
- service-area page
- service and place page
- blog post
- guide
- FAQ section
- Google Business Profile category
- Google Business Profile service
- profile post
- no new asset
- merge or refresh an existing page

One page may target a cluster of related phrases. One phrase does not automatically require one page.

## Step 8: Build the service-area model

Classify places as:

- `primary`: routinely served, operationally important, and close to the business base
- `secondary`: regularly served but lower priority or farther away
- `limited`: served under defined conditions
- `excluded`: not served
- `unknown`: needs client confirmation

Evaluate:

- real drive time
- dispatch capacity
- employee coverage
- licensing
- pricing and travel fees
- emergency availability
- profitability
- seasonal access
- verified work history
- customer demand
- market competition

Google currently allows a service-area business to specify up to 20 service areas and advises that the overall area generally should not extend beyond about two hours of driving time from the business base. Verify current policy before making changes.

A profile service area tells customers where the business operates. It does not guarantee map ranking in every selected place.

## Step 9: Decide whether a location page is justified

A location page requires all of the following:

- the business truly serves the place
- the page has a clear customer purpose
- the business can provide a useful next step
- the page can include unique, approved local information
- the page does not imply a physical office that does not exist
- the page fits a browsable site hierarchy
- the page is not a duplicate with only a place name changed

Strong evidence may include:

- verified jobs in the area
- distinct service availability
- local regulations or conditions
- area-specific scheduling or travel information
- local testimonials with permission
- approved project examples
- real partnerships
- clear demand in Search Console, keyword tools, or live results

Do not create a location page solely because a city is nearby.

## Step 10: Keep blogs in the system

Blog posts remain an approved Local SEO asset.

Use a blog post when the query is educational, comparative, seasonal, regulatory, or decision-focused and a service page would not be the best answer.

Valid local blog purposes include:

- answering a real customer question
- explaining a local rule or permit issue
- addressing a seasonal problem
- comparing repair and replacement choices
- explaining price factors
- documenting a verified local project
- supporting a service or location page
- earning relevant links or citations
- creating material for an approved profile post or email

A weekly publishing cadence is allowed when the client agreement requires it. The topic must still have a useful purpose. Do not publish filler merely to meet a date.

## Step 11: Score priorities

Use a documented scoring model. Suggested factors:

- business fit
- commercial intent
- evidence of demand
- value to the client
- ability to fulfill
- competition
- current visibility
- page gap
- seasonal timing
- effort
- policy risk
- confidence

Example:

```text
priority_score =
  business_fit
  + intent_value
  + evidence_strength
  + fulfillment_fit
  + gap_value
  - effort
  - policy_risk
```

The score helps order work. It does not predict ranking.

## Step 12: Create the strategy output

Include:

- research date
- sources and tool settings
- approved services
- target places
- excluded places
- keyword clusters
- intent
- current asset
- proposed asset
- priority
- evidence
- limitations
- approval needs
- tracking method

## Local ranking limitation statement

Every strategy must state:

- rankings vary by search location and query
- distance cannot be removed through copy or profile settings
- service-area selection does not create a new physical presence
- location pages do not guarantee map pack coverage
- tool volumes and difficulty scores are estimates
- competitor observations do not prove causation
- Google can change result layouts and systems
- no ranking position, lead volume, revenue, or completion date is guaranteed

## Verification

Before approval:

- confirm every service and place
- confirm no fake office is implied
- confirm no city-swapped page plan
- confirm no invented metrics
- confirm each proposed asset has a purpose
- confirm blogs are retained where intent supports them
- confirm the strategy separates facts, observations, and hypotheses
