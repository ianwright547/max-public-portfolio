# Local SEO knowledge pack

## Purpose

This pack gives Max a controlled system for local SEO research, planning, execution, verification, and reporting.

It is split into focused files so Max can load only the knowledge required for the current task. The main skill routes the work. The roadmap SOP decides the phase. The detailed SOPs explain the process. The checklists verify completion. The templates preserve approved facts and consistent output.

The current operating order is:

```text
Rank-map measurement
        ↓
Foundation alignment
        ↓
Trust building and topical expansion when needed
        ↓
Core 30 when the market and data justify it
        ↓
Geographic expansion
        ↓
Sustained measurement and authority work
```

The Universal Human Writing SOP must be loaded before any customer-facing or client-facing copy is finalized.

## Scope

This pack is complete for the main Local SEO workflow used by local service businesses, storefronts, service-area businesses, and multi-location brands.

It covers:

- research
- search intent
- profiles
- service areas
- service, location, and blog content
- content briefs
- website audits
- competitor analysis
- technical checks
- citations
- reviews
- local authority
- internal links
- structured data
- Search Console
- geogrids
- multi-location control
- reporting
- approvals
- verification

Separate knowledge packs may still be needed for ecommerce SEO, international SEO, marketplace SEO, large publisher SEO, or paid search.

## Required context order

Max should assemble Local SEO context in this order:

1. Approved client facts
2. Current task requirements
3. Relevant Local SEO SOPs
4. Approved output template
5. Verification checklist
6. Historical decisions and feedback
7. Universal Human Writing SOP
8. Live or imported evidence

A reusable SOP must never override approved client facts or current platform policy.

## Core operating principles

- Accuracy comes before persuasion.
- A recommendation is not completed work.
- Completed work is not a verified result.
- A ranking observation is not proof of causation.
- Tool estimates must not be presented as measured facts.
- Local visibility must be evaluated by location, query, date, and data source.
- Blog posts remain part of the method when they serve a real search intent or customer need.
- Google Business Profile changes, publication, outreach, review replies, and external submissions require the approval level defined by Max.
- Every important action and result must be recorded with evidence.

## Required prohibitions

Max must not create, recommend, approve, or conceal:

- fake reviews
- fake locations
- keyword stuffing
- unsupported guarantees
- invented rankings
- invented customer results
- misleading business descriptions
- fabricated services, credentials, awards, hours, prices, testimonials, or business history
- false completion claims
- doorway pages that only swap place names
- spammy citation submissions
- link schemes
- hidden customer addresses published without approval
- review gating or incentives that violate platform rules

## Status language

Use these terms consistently:

- `proposed`: recommended but not approved
- `approved`: authorized but not executed
- `in_progress`: execution started
- `executed`: action completed but not yet verified
- `verified`: evidence confirms the intended change
- `observed_result`: a measured outcome after execution
- `attributed_result`: a result supported by enough evidence to connect it to the action
- `rejected`: not approved
- `blocked`: cannot proceed because a dependency or policy issue remains

## Versioning

Save each approved update as a new version.

Do not silently replace prior versions. Record:

- version number
- change summary
- reason for change
- source or policy that caused the change
- approver
- approval time
- affected workflows
